# Wardrobe catalog and styling rules

The demo closet contains 53 illustrative items across tops, bottoms, outerwear, footwear, accessories, and suits. These are sample data only. In a signed-in closet, recommendations use only garments the person has saved. The entry form offers over 80 common piece types and 44 color shortcuts, while retaining editable text for unusual names and shades. Color chips use a shared swatch map across entry, garment art, and recommendation previews.

## Evidence and interpretation

- Gray et al., *The Science of Style: In Fashion, Colors Should Match Only Moderately*, PLOS ONE 9(7), 2014, https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0102772. Their judgments of controlled four-color outfits favored moderate coordination. The application penalizes identical head-to-toe shades and too many saturated accents; the score is a design heuristic, not a reproduction of their experimental model.
- Schloss and Palmer, *Aesthetic response to color combinations: preference, harmony, and similarity*, Attention, Perception, & Psychophysics 73, 2011, https://doi.org/10.3758/s13414-010-0027-0. Preference and harmony are distinct judgments, with hue similarity and lightness contrast contributing differently. The palette score uses hue distance, neutral anchors, and lightness difference rather than a rigid list of approved pairs.
- Zegna, *Su Misura Selection #4*, https://www.zegna.com/content/dam/zegna-commerce/editorials/pdf-files/mtm/MTM_DIGITAL_MAILER_USA.pdf. Its editorial presents navy/gray versatility, brown/burgundy combinations, wool flannel, and layered tailoring. These are inspiration for sample garments and texture/occasion metadata, not an endorsement or an empirical claim.
- Loro Piana, *The Gift of Kings Jacket*, https://kr.loropiana.com/en/man/shirts/the-gift-of-kings-jacket-FAO5276_W000.html. The house describes a loose overshirt layered over a T-shirt or henley and a relaxed yet polished effect. That informs the selection of sample overshirts and layering options.

## Current limits

Color names are mapped to approximate display swatches, not measured fabric color or a user's photo. Unknown colors get a conservative score and a generic swatch. A single accessory is optional, and the generator does not yet combine two tops or both a blazer and an overcoat. The numeric weights and thresholds are editorial heuristics that need user feedback, not a validated personal-style predictor. Formal means a broad formality filter and should not be treated as an event-specific dress code.
